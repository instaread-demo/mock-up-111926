One Week Later: Uncalled Races from the 2026 Midterm Election

By 270toWin Staff

November 10, 2026, 2:57 PM ET

Sponsored by

Instaread

Democrats have won control of both houses of Congress and a majority of governorships. The final tallies will be determined by these remaining uncalled races.

For results by office and state, see the 2026 election results hub.

U.S. Senate

Democrats have ensured a Senate majority in the next Congress by flipping at least seven seats: Iowa, Kansas, Maine, North Carolina, Ohio, South Carolina, and Texas. As of November 10, races in Alaska and Mississippi remain uncalled. 

Polymarket traders give the edge to Democrats in both these contests.

Alaska

Republican Dan Sullivan is seeking a third term. His main challenger is Democrat Mary Peltola. Two minor candidates are also on the ballot, with one sharing the same name as the incumbent. This has caused controversy and spurred legal action by the Department of Justice.

This election will likely be decided by a ranked choice tabulation on November 18.

Mississippi

Few people had this contest on the competitive radar in a state that last elected a Democrat to the Senate in 1982. Scott Colom (D) narrowly leads Cindy Hyde-Smith (R). The GOP incumbent is seeking a second full term.

U.S. House

19 races, including two in Louisiana that will go to a runoff, remain uncalled as of November 10. Three of those (CA-04, CA-07, and CA-40) involve two candidates of the same party. Those races are already included in the total in the House map above.

Democrats seem likely to equal or surpass the 257 seats won in the 2008 election, the most for either party this century.

Alaska At-Large

Incumbent Nick Begich (R) leads the four-person field. If no candidate gets a majority, a ranked choice tabulation will take place on November 18.

California District 4

Democrats are guaranteed to keep the seat; whether incumbent Mike Thompson keeps his job remains to be decided.

California District 7

Democrats are guaranteed to keep the seat. 11-term incumbent Doris Matsui seems likely to prevail. 

California District 23

Republicans have become scarce in California's U.S. House delegation. If Jay Obernolte (R) prevails in his quest for a 4th term, he'll be one of only four in 2027 across the state's 52 districts.

California District 40

While Republicans are guaranteed to keep the seat, one incumbent will lose their job. This member vs. member clash was forced by midcycle redistricting.

Colorado District 4

In 2024, Lauren Boebert (R) switched to this 'safer' district from District 3. While that effort was initially successful, it will probably not work out this time around. 

Florida District 27

Maria Salazar (R) is looking to win a 4th term in a closer-than-expected contest. 

To accommodate midcycle redistricting, Louisiana delayed its planned move to partisan primaries for U.S. House. The Election Day contests involved all candidates from all parties. In Districts 5 and 6, no candidate received a majority. The top two finishers in each will meet on December 12 to determine a winner. 

Louisiana District 5

Julia Letlow (R) retired to run for U.S. Senate. Republican candidates received over 60% of the vote in this nine-person field. Gabe Firment (R) will be favored in the runoff.

Louisiana District 6

This district was most affected by redistricting, shifting some 45 points to the right. As such, incumbent Cleo Fields (D) chose to retire. Blake Miguez (R) will be favored in the runoff.

Maine District 2

Former Gov. Paul LePage (R) has built a lead of about 1.6% over Matt Dunlap (D) as this article is published. The winner will succeed retiring Democrat Jared Golden in a district that voted for Donald Trump by 9% in 2024. Outside of changes due to midcycle redistricting, this is the most Republican district in the country currently represented by a Democrat.

Minnesota District 1

It took a while for the forecasters to catch up to the prediction markets on this one. While largely seen as 'safe' for incumbent Brad Finstad (R) until the closing weeks, the markets saw a more competitive race. That is how it has played out, with the two virtually tied.

In 2022, both parties won seven U.S. House seats in battleground North Carolina. Redistricting by the Republican-led Legislature gained the party another three seats in 2024. This year, an additional effort made District 1 more conservative in an effort to flip that seat. This repeated gerrymandering worked well - until it didn't.

As of November 10, Democrats have flipped five of the 10 GOP-held seats and the remaining five remain uncalled.

North Carolina District 3

This district was made eight points less conservative in the failed effort to defeat Don Davis in District 1. While it remained Trump +14, Greg Murphy (R) is in a virtual tie with his Democratic challenger.

North Carolina District 5

Virginia Foxx (R) won an 11th term by 19% in 2024. She is also in a virtual tie with the Democratic nominee.

North Carolina District 7

David Rouzer (R) won a 6th term by 17% in 2024. Based on Polymarket traders, he is likely to narrowly hold the seat.

North Carolina District 8

Freshman Mark Harris (R) won by 19% in 2024. Based on Polymarket traders, he is likely to narrowly hold the seat.

North Carolina District 10

A similar story here, where Pat Harrigan (R) won by 19% in 2024. Based on Polymarket traders, he is likely to narrowly hold the seat.

New York District 1

Both Long Island districts remain uncalled. In the North Shore District 1, Nicholas LaLota (R) has the lead as he seeks a third term.

New York District 2

On the South Shore of Long Island, Andrew Garbarino (R) leads as he seeks a 4th term.

Tennessee District 4

Republicans implemented midcycle redistricting to win District 9 (Memphis) and create a 9-0 map. To make District 9 more conservative, conservative areas of other districts were 'annexed'. District 4 moved from Trump +43% to Trump +23%. While this was on few radars, that move may have backfired, as Scott DesJarlais (R) trails.

Governor

Democrats flipped seats in Georgia, Iowa, Nevada, Ohio, and Texas. Regardless of the outcome of the two uncalled races, the party will hold the governor's office in a majority of states for the first time since 2010.

Alaska

Mike Dunleavy (R) was ineligible to run for a third term. While the total party vote is split just about 50/50, there are three Republicans splitting that party's vote, while Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins has earned all the Democratic vote. Kreiss-Tomkins is just short of the 50% needed to avoid a ranked choice tabulation on November 18.

Kansas

Laura Kelly (D) was ineligible due to term limits. This is a very close contest between Cindy Holscher (D) and Ty Masterson (R).
