    Donald Trump is getting increasingly pally with Russian President Vladimir Putin.

    Vladimir Putin has left his chat with Donald Trump with every reason to feel buoyant about Russia and US relations. For the Russian President, a three-way meeting with Trump and Xi Jinping presents the opportunity to demonstrate that he heads a superpower on a par with the United States and China. While dealing another blow to Western efforts to isolate Putin, it also gives him an opportunity to try to persuade Trump to lean into Moscow's demands for a settlement in Ukraine at a time when Washington wants to broker a peace deal.

    One three-word comment from Trump suggests it's mission accomplished from the exchange in China. "He's my friend," the US President boasted. “We’ve had a great relationship for many years,” he also said.

    It was the first time the leaders have held talks since their discussions in Alaska last year that failed to broker a deal to ease Russia’s war in Ukraine, the Associated Press reports. “Wonderful things can come out of this — or at least start,” Mr Trump said after joining Mr Putin for the one-on-one meeting on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Co-operation (APEC) Summit.

    Their meeting lasted roughly 40 minutes. The current occupant of the White House has frequently boasted of his rapport with Mr Putin, as relations between the US and Ukraine appear to have cratered recently.

    Mr Trump reached an oil deal with the Kremlin, while Kyiv was undermined by disruptions in satellite support for its drones. The President has said he wants to talk to Xi and Putin leaders about their nuclear stockpiles.

    The last nuclear arms pact between Russia and the United States, known as New Start, expired in February, and Mr Trump has said he wants “a new, improved and modernised” deal that includes China. But it is thought that China has less motivation to accept limits, given that its arsenal of more than 600 warheads is far smaller than the more than 5,000 warheads believed to be in each of the US and Russian stockpiles.
