Battle Royale vs Traditional Multiplayer Market Share In 2026

ByRonny Alexander November 17, 2026
Listen to audio version of this article

Battle royale playtime on PC and console fell 27% in 2025, per Newzoo, while total playtime on those platforms slipped 1%. Counter-Strike 2 alone took 7% of PC playtime that year; Apex Legends took 0.9%.

This breakdown of battle royale vs traditional multiplayer market share covers playtime, Steam concurrency, mobile revenue, streaming, esports and publisher earnings, with data through November 2026.

How Much PC Playtime Goes to Battle Royale vs Traditional Multiplayer?

Share of total PC playtime by title, 2025 (%)

Battle royaleTraditional multiplayerOther formats

Counter-Strike 2 (7%) and League of Legends (6.9%) each took more PC playtime in 2025 than Fortnite (4.3%), according to Newzoo.

Dota 2 held 3.6% and Valorant 3.2%. Apex Legends sat at 0.9%, below Escape from Tarkov, an extraction shooter, at 1.7%.

Fortnite’s 4.3% still made it the fifth most-played PC title. More on its audience is in our Fortnite player data breakdown.

Call of Duty (1.8%) spans both battle royale and traditional modes, so it is grouped separately above.

Source: Newzoo PC & Console Gaming Report 2026, full-year 2025, 37 markets excluding China and India

27% Less Battle Royale Playtime in 2025, 5% Less for Shooters

Newzoo measured a 27% year-over-year drop in battle royale playtime across PC, PlayStation and Xbox in 2025. Fortnite lost 29% and Apex Legends 24%.

Playtime change on PC and console, 2025 vs 2024 (%)

Shooters as a genre fell 5%. Sandbox, which includes Roblox’s fast-growing platform and Minecraft, rose 36%.

Call of Duty playtime fell 25.5% on PlayStation and 59.4% on PC. Those declines also cover the franchise’s free-to-play mode, tracked in our Call of Duty: Warzone statistics.

By platform, PC playtime rose 3%, Xbox fell 3% and PlayStation fell 4%.

Source: Newzoo PC & Console Gaming Report 2026, full-year 2025

Free-to-Play Revenue Shifted Toward PC

PC was the only platform where free-to-play monetization improved in 2025. Revenue per hour played rose 10%, running at about twice the PlayStation level and three times Xbox.

Free-to-play revenue on PlayStation fell 8.2%. Fortnite on PC earned 1.7 times more per hour played than a year earlier, despite lower playtime.

Newzoo’s Free-to-play in 2026 study, released in October 2026, put free-to-play titles at 29.3% of measured PC, PlayStation and Xbox playtime in 2025, with Roblox and Fortnite counted separately.

Source: Newzoo PC & Console Gaming Report 2026 and Free-to-play in 2026, 2025 data (revenue: US, UK, Germany, France, Spain, Italy)

Is Battle Royale Losing Streaming Share to Traditional Shooters?

Battle royale share of all gaming hours watched, H1 2026

11%

Battle royale drew just under 2 billion hours watched in H1 2026, in a Hatchet dataset that includes TikTok LIVE. Garena Free Fire led with 601 million hours.

Free Fire, PUBG Mobile and Battlegrounds Mobile India passed 1.1 billion hours combined. Fortnite, Apex Legends, PUBG: Battlegrounds and Call of Duty: Warzone totaled about 821 million.

Source: Hatchet, H1 2026

Battle royale hours watched per quarter, excluding TikTok LIVE (millions)

Quarterly battle royale viewership peaked at 1.7 billion hours in Q2 and Q3 2020. It bottomed at 340 million in Q4 2025, then reached 529 million in Q2 2026.

Other shooter-type genres slid from 3.3 billion hours in Q2 2020 to 1.9 billion in Q2 2026.

Source: Hatchet, Q2 2020 to Q2 2026, excluding TikTok LIVE

Battle Royale Share of Shooter Viewership

Without TikTok LIVE, battle royale held 37-39% of shooter-type viewership in 2019 and 2020, 25% by early 2023 and 14% in Q4 2025. With TikTok LIVE included, its H1 2026 share was 37%.

Hatchet noted the Q4 2025 low coincided with ARC Raiders’ October 2025 launch. The extraction shooter logged 251 million hours in H1 2026.

Unique channels streaming battle royale fell from 5.7 million in Q1 2021 to 775,000 in Q4 2025, then climbed to 2.0 million in Q2 2026.

Source: Hatchet, Battle Royale Resurgence on Live Streaming, August 2026

Esports Peak Viewers: PUBG Mobile +83%, League of Legends Still Ahead

Peak viewers by tournament edition, 2024 vs 2025

All five major battle royale tournaments raised their peak viewership from 2024 to 2025. The PUBG Mobile Global Championship climbed from 827,000 to 1.5 million.

The PUBG Mobile World Cup rose 73%, the Free Fire World Series 15%, the Fortnite Champion Series 14% and the Apex Legends Global Series 10%.

Source: Hatchet, 2024 and 2025 tournament editions

Traditional titles still set the ceiling. League of Legends’ Worlds 2025 peaked at about 6.7 million viewers, excluding Chinese platforms.

Counter-Strike’s BLAST.tv Austin Major 2025 peaked at 1.79 million viewers.

Source: Esports Charts, 2025 peak viewers

FAQs

Is battle royale dying, as many Reddit threads claim?

Not by streaming data. Battle royale drew just under 2 billion hours watched in H1 2026, 11% of all gaming viewership (Hatchet). Playtime is shrinking, though: Newzoo recorded a 27% drop on PC and console in 2025.

Which battle royale game has the most players on Steam?

PUBG: Battlegrounds, with a 24-hour peak of 713,683 concurrent players on October 11, 2026, per SteamDB. In Steam’s January 5, 2026 snapshot it had 709,468, against 174,089 for Apex Legends.

Why did battle royale playtime drop in 2025?

No single cause is proven. Newzoo recorded sandbox playtime up 36% as battle royale fell 27%. Hatchet noted the genre’s Q4 2025 viewership low coincided with ARC Raiders’ October 2025 launch.

Do MOBAs earn more than battle royale games on mobile?

At the top, yes. Honor of Kings, a MOBA, led 2025 mobile IAP revenue at $1.68 billion, per AppMagic. PUBG Mobile ranked 7th with over $1 billion, and Free Fire: Winterlands earned $366 million.

What share of PC playtime does Fortnite have?

Fortnite took 4.3% of PC playtime in 2025, Newzoo reports. That trails Counter-Strike 2 at 7% and League of Legends at 6.9%, while Apex Legends held 0.9%.
