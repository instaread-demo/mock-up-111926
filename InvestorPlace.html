    5 AI Stocks That Could Win After Huang’s Nvidia Exit

    Three InvestorPlace experts share where they see risk and opportunity as the fight for AI spending heats up.

    16h ago · November 18, 2026 By InvestorPlace Staff

    Listen to the audio version of this article (generated by AI).

    Listen to audio version of this article

    After 33 years running NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA), Jensen Huang has stepped down. For the companies that have spent years trying to win business away from Nvidia, his exit may be the opening they’ve been waiting for.

    Luke Lango put it bluntly on the November 17 episode of The Investor Place: “Without that leather shield, I think those efforts can make a lot more headway.”

    The “leather shield” is Huang himself. Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD), Amazon.com, Inc. (AMZN) and Google parent Alphabet Inc. (GOOGL) have all built alternatives to Nvidia’s chips, yet Luke argues Huang’s charisma at the negotiating table was one of the reasons the biggest buyers kept coming back. With CFO Colette Kress now serving as interim CEO, he thinks those buyers will start taking a harder look at their options.

    Luke, Louis Navellier and Eric Fry each see a different way to play it. Luke named five stocks he thinks could benefit. Louis is holding on to Nvidia. And Eric is focused on the companies putting AI to work.

    Editor’s note: This episode was recorded before Nvidia reported its fiscal third-quarter 2027 results on November 17, 2026. Comments about the upcoming report reflect the panelists’ expectations at the time of filming.

    What Huang’s Exit Means for Nvidia and the AI Trade

    All three panelists expect Nvidia’s near-term business to hold up. “The 2027 road map is locked in,” Luke said. “Nothing’s going to change in the 2027 numbers.” Louis was even more direct: “We got four years of orders.” Even Eric, the most cautious of the three, said 2027 demand is “baked into the cake.”

    The uncertainty starts in 2028. That’s when big customers make their next round of purchasing decisions, and Luke believes rivals will push harder without Huang across the table. Louis pointed to the advantage Nvidia keeps regardless of who runs it: CUDA, the software developers have spent years writing for Nvidia’s chips. Replacing the hardware means replacing that software too, and no competitor offers an equal substitute yet.

    Eric is more worried about money than chips. Nvidia has lent heavily to its own customers, and he noted reporting that Kress has been less enthusiastic than Huang about extending that credit. “Nvidia has been called the central bank of AI, right? Because of its ability to finance its own customers,” Eric said. If that support pulls back, he sees it as “very, very negative for the ecosystem broadly.”

    The panel saw the market’s reaction as a sign investors share that worry. On the day Huang left, CoreWeave, Inc. (CRWV), Super Micro Computer, Inc. (SMCI) and Dell Technologies Inc. (DELL) all fell further than Nvidia did.

    Nvidia’s first report without Huang came out after filming. The company posted fiscal third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier, and guided to about $130 billion for the fourth quarter, plus or minus 3% (Nvidia, November 17, 2026).

    Luke Lango’s 5 AI Stocks to Watch Beyond Nvidia

    Luke’s case comes down to market share. By his estimate, Nvidia controls roughly 85% of the AI chip market today and builds much of the surrounding data center gear, like networking and optics, in-house. If that share slips to “let’s say, mid-60s,” he said, “that 20 points translates downstream through the whole chain.”

    That’s why the stocks that rallied the day Huang left went well beyond AMD. Luke is bullish on three chipmakers and sees two more opportunities in networking and data center hardware. Here they are, starting with his favorite.

    Broadcom: Luke’s Top Pick

    Broadcom Inc. (AVGO) designs custom AI chips for the biggest cloud companies. Luke said he’s bullish on all three chipmakers, but Broadcom stands out because “the custom silicon projects are the ones that are going to accelerate the most as a result of the Nvidia situation.”

    He also likes how Broadcom holds up if AI spending cools. Luke listed several pressures on 2027 and 2028 budgets: widening debt spreads, a rising 10-year Treasury yield, supply shortages and political pushback against data center construction. If the big spenders pull back, he expects them to lean harder on their own chip programs, which is exactly where Broadcom works. In his words, “Broadcom’s actually in a pretty good position regardless of which way the wind blows in ’27.”

    Advanced Micro Devices: The Headline Challenger

    Luke called AMD the “headline winner” if Nvidia loses ground. He credited CEO Lisa Su with “a really good product roadmap” and pointed to AMD’s recent deals with Anthropic and Amazon.

    He admits AMD is still behind. Every competitor is. But Huang’s exit gives buyers a reason to rethink their 2028 plans, and Luke thinks Su now carries more weight in negotiations than whoever Nvidia puts in front of customers.

    Marvell Technology: Another Custom Silicon Play

    Marvell Technology, Inc. (MRVL) works with large customers on custom chips, much like Broadcom. Luke grouped it with Broadcom as a custom silicon partner that rallied the day Huang was pushed out, and he’s bullish on it, though Broadcom remains his preferred way to play the trend.

    Arista Networks: A Networking Opening

    Arista Networks, Inc. (ANET) sells the networking equipment that connects servers in large data centers. Customers who buy Nvidia’s systems often take Nvidia’s networking along with them. If they shift some purchases elsewhere, Luke expects more of that business to go to “non-Nvidia networking,” which is Arista’s specialty.

    Celestica: Strong Backlog, Weak Grade

    Celestica Inc. (CLS) builds data center hardware, and Luke counted it among the stocks that did well the day Huang left. “Celestica’s in that game,” he said.

    Louis owns it, though with a caveat. Celestica “slipped to a D-rating in Stock Grader,” he said, “but the fundamentals were so good and the backlog was so good, we held it and we’re happy to see it’s now rallying.” He hopes to see the grade climb to a C soon.

    Louis Navellier Is Still Holding Nvidia Stock

    Nvidia is “our largest holding actually in terms of what we own,” Louis said. Part of the reason is that the position grew so much and he didn’t want to pay the taxes on selling it. But he also likes what he sees. Louis cited margins near 70%, a growing order backlog and what he called a low price-to-earnings ratio. He called it “a very safe hold here near term.”

    He’s clear about what would change his mind. Louis said three things would be warning signs: analysts cutting earnings estimates, margins coming under pressure or the order backlog peaking.

    Eric Fry Is Looking Beyond the AI Builders

    Eric sees the AI trade “widening out” rather than breaking down. He has been looking to pivot from companies that build AI, like Nvidia, toward companies that use it. He admits the market hasn’t rewarded that shift yet: the two groups have performed about the same. And he’s not telling anyone to dump the builders. “You can still own a lot of builders,” he said.

    Still, Huang’s exit doesn’t change where he’s hunting. “I’m not looking for opportunity close to that epicenter,” Eric said. His focus is biopharma and medical technology. “I’m going to just keep banging that drum because I think that’s the best, most exciting area to be looking.”

    What Investors Should Watch Next

    The biggest open question is who runs Nvidia next. Luke thinks the most likely candidate is Rene Haas, CEO of Arm Holdings plc (ARM), who spent seven years at Nvidia, and he called that “a hire that would really help the stock.” Some analysts argue Nvidia is worth 25% less without Huang. Luke said that could prove true if the new CEO can’t protect Nvidia’s position in 2028 and beyond.

    After that, watch where customers spend. If the big cloud companies keep committing to Nvidia, its lead likely holds. If more of their budgets go to custom chips and outside networking, Luke’s five stocks could benefit. And if Nvidia gets stingier with customer financing, Eric’s concern about the broader AI ecosystem moves to the front.

    Which AI stock does Luke Lango like most after Jensen Huang’s exit?

    Broadcom. Luke is also bullish on AMD and Marvell, but he believes Broadcom’s custom chip business stands to gain the most and holds up best if AI spending slows.

    Could AMD take market share from Nvidia?

    Luke thinks so, mainly from 2028 on. AMD has a strong product roadmap and recent deals with Anthropic and Amazon. Nvidia’s CUDA software remains a major hurdle for any competitor.

    Is Louis Navellier selling Nvidia stock?

    No. Louis said Nvidia is the largest holding in what his team owns. He plans to keep it unless analysts cut estimates, margins shrink or the order backlog peaks.
