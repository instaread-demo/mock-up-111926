Pending Home Sales Hit Another Record Low As High Mortgage Rates Continue to Sting

  • by Colin Robertson
  • November 18, 2026November 18, 2026

The housing market is really starting to show the effects of sticky mortgage rates in the mid-7s.

For the second consecutive month, pending home sales hit a fresh record low, according to the National Association of Realtors (NAR).

And that could lead to the lowest actual existing-home sales tally since the mid-1990s, per NAR chief economist Lawrence Yun.

However, in the news release he also noted that mortgage rates may have peaked, so perhaps we’re seeing the worst of it.

The question though, is if they have indeed peaked, when/how will they actually fall again?

Pending Home Sales Hit Another Record Low in October as Mortgage Rates Deter Buyers

Pending home sales fell 4.7% in October and 16.7% from a year ago, the lowest reading in the NAR index’s 25-year history.

Worse still, it was the second consecutive record low following the one seen in September.

Pending sales count contracts signed but not yet closed, so they point to weak actual sales over the next couple months.

As a result, NAR’s chief economist Yun said annual existing-home sales could fall to the lowest levels since the mid-1990s.

Consider that the U.S. population has grown nearly 40% since then and the numbers are pretty bleak.

The takeaway here is that the housing market is basically frozen, with prospective home buyers balking at the combination of high price and high interest rate.

To make matters worse, the Fed is still expected to hike its own federal funds rate next month, and potentially twice more after that.

In addition, single-family permits, which show what builders plan to start, plummeted 8.7% to the lowest level since early 2023, per the Commerce Department.

So despite the poor sales volume, home prices have held up better than expected, due in part to less new home building and also mortgage rate lock-in keeping existing home supply in check.

Nvidia Beat Expectations, But Stocks Continue to Fall

The other big piece of news was Nvidia’s earnings, which were an expected beat (and raised outlook).

Despite that, the stock fell another three percent today and the wider stock market continued its march lower.

The one piece of good news was bond yields actually came down about five basis points, with the 10-year yield back around 5.05%.

It’s not going to move mortgage rates much, but it will result in slightly lower rates versus yesterday and the day before.

However, we’re talking basis points, probably not even an eighth lower. So a rate quote of 7.50% might still be 7.50%, but with fewer closing costs or less mortgage discount points.

This all points to more pain ahead, not just for prospective home buyers (and sellers), but also mortgage lenders attempting to navigate this weak sales environment.

Rate Hikes Expected to Keep Coming to Combat Sticky Inflation

While the stock market stumbles, inflation continues to be the elephant in the room.

We’ve gotten hot report after hot economic report lately that has pointed to lasting high prices, with the only solution seemingly more Fed rate hikes.

Speaking of, Fed governor Christopher Waller said the Fed may need to “shock people” with a 50-bp rate hike next month, though back-to-back 25-bp hikes will likely be the move.

He added that the central bank has to effectively reverse the three 25-point rate hikes from last year and may need to do even more than that.

None of that bodes well for mortgage rates, though a weak economic report and/or progress on Iran remain the levers to see relief.

So there are still paths to lower mortgage rates, but it feels like it’s getting harder.
