Who decides when your team plays? How the football fixture list is made

By 11v11 Site Editor , 17th November 2026 at 3:28 PM (11v11 blog)

Have you ever wondered how the fixtures are put together each season?

Most fans have an excellent grasp of other elements in football such as how online sports betting works or how to find live streams of matches.

However, many supporters are probably unaware of who decides when their team plays. With that in mind, read on as we look at how the football fixture list is made.

How are the fixtures compiled?

For the purposes of this guide, we will use the English Premier League as the reference point.

Each season is made up of 380 fixtures, with the 20 clubs playing each other once home and away during the campaign.

The exact dates fluctuate each season due to major tournaments such as the World Cup or European Championship. Fixtures traditionally start in August and end in May.

Premier League fixtures are tied in with other domestic competitions including the English Football League’s three divisions – Championship, League One and League Two.

The FA Cup, League Cup, Champions League, Europa League, Europa Conference League and FIFA’s international match calendar also influence the fixtures.

Glenn Thompson, of information technology services company Atos, plays a key role in determining when the 2,036 fixtures in the Premier League and the three EPL divisions are to be played.

The dates for the European competitions and FIFA calendar are ringfenced, before the domestic cup competitions are factored into the equation.

For the 2026/27 season, Thompson and the Premier League were left with 33 weekends and five midweeks where the fixtures could be slotted into.

What factors are taken into consideration?

Clubs are generally asked in April to highlight any specific dates they wish to play at home or away the following season.

The requests are linked to elements such as club anniversaries, stadium redevelopments or if other events are scheduled to be staged at their ground.

Clubs located close to each other – such as Everton and Liverpool or Manchester United and Manchester City – are given alternative home and away fixtures to take the strain off local police.

Once the information has been inputted into the fixture computer, the fixtures will be determined based on the following principles:

  • There should be a split of three home fixtures and two away, or two home fixtures and three away, in any five matches.
  • A team will never have more than two home or away matches in a row.
  • Wherever possible, teams will be home and away either side of cup ties and international breaks.
  • A club will not start or finish the season with two home or two away matches.
  • Clubs should be given a fair split of home and away fixtures over the festive season.

The fixtures computer also takes travel for fans into account, particularly on Boxing Day, New Year’s Date or other noteworthy dates.

Where possible, the computer will ensure that fans from the same areas are not travelling to away matches on the same transport routes.

Once the fixtures have been formulated, they are reviewed by stakeholders to ensure that any anomalies are ironed out before they are released.

Changing just one fixture can have a significant impact on the rest of the list, so it is imperative that the initial criteria entered into the computer is as accurate as possible.

After the fixtures have been published, changes are made based on which games are selected for live coverage by broadcasters such as Sky Sports and TNT Sports.

Fans have become accustomed to being flexible, as fixtures originally scheduled for 3.00 pm on Saturday can be moved to a different time or day.
