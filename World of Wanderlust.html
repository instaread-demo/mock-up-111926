The 20 Most Beautiful Cities in the World

Some cities are useful, some are beautiful, and a handful are both; the most beautiful cities in the world are the ones that manage all of it at once. The most beautiful cities in the world are the ones where the setting does half the work: a harbour, a river, a mountain or a lagoon that the buildings have had centuries to grow into. Above all, they are the cities you keep going back to.

This list ranks the 20 most beautiful cities in the world, grouped by continent so you can plan around them. For each one you will find why it earns its place, the one thing not to miss, where to stay and how to arrive. In short, it is the list for the long-haul trip you have been putting off.

If you want to stay closer to home, our 30 most beautiful cities in Europe goes deeper on one continent. For the full bucket list, there is 101 cities to visit before you die.

Quick picks: the most beautiful cities in the world for your kind of trip

Short on time? Then choose by what you want from the trip. Each pick links down to its full entry.

How the continents fit together. Europe (cities 1 to 7) is the easiest to combine, because the trains link them. The Americas (8 to 12) span two continents and need flights between each. Asia (13 to 17) works as a loop from Singapore or Hong Kong. Finally, Africa and Australia (18 to 20) are each a trip in their own right, and in many ways they are the most beautiful of all.

The most beautiful cities in the world: Europe

Europe has more beautiful cities per square kilometre than anywhere else, so narrowing it to seven was the hardest part of this list. These are the ones that still stop you in the street.

1. Paris, France

Paris is the city every other city is measured against, and it has earned it. Haussmann’s boulevards, the bridges over the Seine, the grey zinc roofs and the Eiffel Tower appearing at the end of every street: in fact, nothing about it is accidental. It is also, even after 20 visits, still surprising.

Do not miss: sunrise at the Trocadéro, the view from the top of the Arc de Triomphe, a walk along the Seine from Notre-Dame to the Musée d’Orsay and, above all, the 30 hidden locations in Paris most visitors never find.

Stay: Le Bristol, on the Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré, is the grandest address in the city. Our 12 best luxury hotels in Paris has the rest.

Getting there: Charles de Gaulle airport is 45 minutes from the centre by RER or taxi. Alternatively, the Eurostar from London takes 2 hours 15.

2. Venice, Italy

Venice is a city built on 118 islands in a lagoon, with canals for streets and Gothic palaces for walls, and it is the most improbable place on earth. During the day it is crowded. By night, however, when the day-trippers leave, it becomes something else: silent, dim and entirely its own.

Do not miss: the Doge’s Palace, the view from the Campanile and the quiet sestieri of Cannaregio and Castello. Then, see our Venice bucket list.

Stay: Aman Venice, in a palazzo on the Grand Canal, is the splurge. Alternatively, Ca’ Maria Adele near the Salute church is the small, romantic choice.

Getting there: 20 minutes by water taxi from Marco Polo airport, which is the only way to arrive. By train, meanwhile, it is 2 hours 15 from Milan.

3. Prague, Czech Republic

Prague survived the 20th century almost untouched, so it still has a Gothic old town, a baroque castle district and a 14th-century bridge lined with statues, all within a twenty-minute walk. In winter, under snow and lit by gas lamps, it is the most beautiful city in Central Europe.

Do not miss: Charles Bridge at dawn, the view from Letná park, the Old Town Square clock on the hour and the best views of Prague, which are mostly free.

Stay: Four Seasons Hotel Prague, on the river facing the castle, has the view. The Augustine, in a 13th-century monastery below the castle, has the atmosphere, meanwhile.

Getting there: Václav Havel airport is 30 minutes from the centre. By train, Vienna is 4 hours and Berlin 4 hours 30.

4. Istanbul, Türkiye

Istanbul is the only city in the world on two continents, and it has been the capital of three empires. Indeed, the skyline of domes and minarets over the Golden Horn has not changed in 500 years. Meanwhile, the ferries, the bazaars and the Bosphorus mansions give it an energy no other city on this list can match.

Do not miss: Hagia Sophia and the Blue Mosque at opening time, the Basilica Cistern, a ferry up the Bosphorus to the Black Sea and a Turkish breakfast on a rooftop in Karaköy.

Stay: Four Seasons Hotel Istanbul at Sultanahmet, in a former prison beside the Hagia Sophia, is the one for the old city. Otherwise, Çırağan Palace Kempinski, on the Bosphorus, is the one for the view.

Getting there: Istanbul airport is 45 minutes to Sultanahmet by taxi or metro. Above all, stay on the European side for a first visit.

5. Edinburgh, Scotland

Edinburgh is a castle on a volcanic rock, a medieval old town tumbling down the ridge beside it and a Georgian new town laid out in grid below, with a mountain called Arthur’s Seat in the middle of it all. It is dramatic, dark and beautiful, especially in winter. In August, moreover, it hosts the biggest arts festival in the world.

Do not miss: the view from Calton Hill at sunset, the climb up Arthur’s Seat, the Royal Mile closes and a whisky in a bar on the Grassmarket.

Stay: The Balmoral, under its clock tower on Princes Street, is the grand classic. Alternatively, Gleneagles Townhouse, in a former bank on St Andrew Square, is the stylish newcomer.

Getting there: Edinburgh airport is 30 minutes from the centre by tram. From London, meanwhile, the train takes 4 hours 20.

6. Amsterdam, Netherlands

Amsterdam is 165 canals, 1,500 bridges and thousands of 17th-century merchants’ houses leaning at odd angles over the water, all crossed by bicycle. It is flat, small and human-scaled, and above all it looks best in the low light of autumn. In spring, meanwhile, the tulips arrive.

Do not miss: a canal boat at dusk, the Rijksmuseum, the Jordaan on a Saturday and the Nine Streets. Our solo guide to Amsterdam has the rest.

Stay: Pulitzer Amsterdam, in 25 canal houses on the Prinsengracht, is the one to book. Otherwise, Hotel De L’Europe, on the Amstel, is the grand alternative.

Getting there: Schiphol airport is 15 minutes from Centraal station by train. Paris and Brussels are under 3 hours 30 by high-speed rail.

7. Lisbon, Portugal

Lisbon is built on seven hills above a river wide enough to look like the sea, with yellow trams climbing between tiled facades and a viewpoint at the top of every street. It has the light of a southern city and, equally, the price of a smaller one. It is also, right now, the most exciting city in Europe to eat in.

Do not miss: the Alfama at dawn, the Jerónimos Monastery and the pastéis de nata in Belém, and sunset at the Miradouro da Senhora do Monte. See our Lisbon bucket list for more.

Stay: Four Seasons Hotel Ritz Lisbon, above the Eduardo VII park, is the grand address. Bairro Alto Hotel, in Chiado, meanwhile, has the best rooftop in the city.

Getting there: Lisbon airport is 20 minutes from the centre by metro. Porto is 3 hours by train, and Sintra is 40 minutes.

The most beautiful cities in the world: the Americas

The Americas have the big skylines and the big settings. These five run from the Arctic-fringed Pacific to the Caribbean, and each one needs a flight from the last.

8. New York City, USA

New York is not pretty, and it is not meant to be. It is beautiful the way a mountain range is beautiful: the Manhattan skyline from the Brooklyn Bridge at dusk, the canyon of Fifth Avenue in snow, Central Park from the top of a tower. In fact, no other city on this list is as thrilling to simply arrive in.

Do not miss: the Brooklyn Bridge walk at sunset, the Top of the Rock, a Sunday in Central Park and the High Line. Then, work through our best bagels in NYC and best secret bars in New York.

Stay: The Bowery Hotel, which we reviewed in our Bowery Hotel review, is the downtown choice. Alternatively, The Carlyle, on the Upper East Side, is the uptown classic.

Getting there: JFK is 45 to 60 minutes from Manhattan by taxi or AirTrain and subway. Newark, however, is often faster for midtown.

9. Rio de Janeiro, Brazil

Rio de Janeiro has the most beautiful setting of any big city on earth: granite peaks rising straight out of the Atlantic, rainforest in the middle of town and a four-kilometre beach as the main street. Then add the statue of Christ on the highest point, and it is impossible to compete with. Yes, it has problems, but at sunset on Ipanema none of them matter.

Do not miss: sunrise at Christ the Redeemer before the crowds, the cable car up Sugarloaf at sunset, a caipirinha at Arpoador rock and a walk through the Santa Teresa neighbourhood.

Stay: Copacabana Palace, a Belmond hotel on the beach since 1923, is the only answer. Otherwise, Hotel Fasano Rio, on Ipanema with a rooftop pool, is the modern alternative.

Getting there: Galeão airport is 45 minutes from the Zona Sul. Stay in Ipanema or Leblon, and use Uber rather than street taxis.

10. San Francisco, USA

San Francisco is 43 hills, a red bridge disappearing into fog, painted Victorian houses and cable cars, all wrapped around the most beautiful bay in America. It is small enough to walk in a day and, at the same time, varied enough to fill a week. Above all, the light is unlike anywhere else in the country.

Do not miss: the Golden Gate Bridge from Crissy Field, the cable car down Hyde Street, Alamo Square’s Painted Ladies and a sunset at Lands End. Our guide to San Francisco has the stays.

Stay: Hotel Drisco, a 1903 mansion in Pacific Heights, is the quiet, elegant choice. Alternatively, The Fairmont, on Nob Hill, is the grand one.

Getting there: SFO is 30 minutes from downtown by BART or taxi. Then, combine it with a drive down the Pacific Coast Highway.

11. Cartagena, Colombia

Cartagena is a walled Spanish colonial city on the Caribbean, painted in ochre, pink and blue, with bougainvillea pouring off wooden balconies and salsa drifting out of the squares at night. In short, it is the most beautiful old town in the Americas. It is also hot, so sightsee early and swim in the afternoon.

Do not miss: a walk along the walls at sunset, the Getsemaní neighbourhood after dark, the Palace of the Inquisition and a boat day to the Rosario Islands.

Stay: Sofitel Legend Santa Clara, in a 17th-century convent inside the walls, is the classic. Otherwise, Casa San Agustín, a short walk away, is the boutique choice.

Getting there: Rafael Núñez airport is 15 minutes from the walled city. In addition, direct flights run from Miami, New York and most of South America.

12. Vancouver, Canada

Vancouver is glass towers on a peninsula between the Pacific and snow-capped mountains, with a 400-hectare rainforest park on one side of downtown and a working harbour on the other. For example, you can ski in the morning and sail in the afternoon. It is also the gateway to the Rockies and Vancouver Island.

Do not miss: the seawall around Stanley Park by bike, Granville Island market, the Capilano suspension bridge and a sunset at English Bay. Our complete guide to Vancouver has more.

Stay: Fairmont Pacific Rim, on the harbour by the convention centre, has the mountain views. Alternatively, Rosewood Hotel Georgia, downtown, is the 1927 classic.

Getting there: YVR airport is 25 minutes from downtown by SkyTrain. Whistler is 2 hours by road, while Victoria is 90 minutes by ferry.

The most beautiful cities in the world: Asia

Asia’s beautiful cities are either very old or very new, and sometimes both at once. These five run from Kyoto’s temples to Singapore’s gardens in the sky, and a loop through them is one of the great trips.

13. Kyoto, Japan

Kyoto was Japan’s capital for a thousand years, and it still has 1,600 temples, 400 shrines, 17 UNESCO sites and the country’s most beautiful gardens. In autumn, when the maples turn, and in April, when the cherry blossom falls, it is the most beautiful city in Asia. It is also calm in a way that Tokyo never is.

Do not miss: Fushimi Inari at dawn, the Philosopher’s Path, the moss garden at Saihō-ji, which must be booked ahead, and an evening walk through Gion. Our Kyoto city guide has the full plan.

Stay: Hoshinoya Kyoto, reached by boat up the Hozu river in Arashiyama, is the most romantic hotel in Japan. Otherwise, Park Hyatt Kyoto, in Higashiyama, is the city alternative.

Getting there: 75 minutes from Osaka Kansai airport by Haruka express, or 2 hours 15 from Tokyo by Shinkansen. See our Japan in 7 days itinerary.

14. Hong Kong, China

Hong Kong is the greatest skyline on earth, pressed between a mountain and a harbour, with jungle hiking trails starting at the end of the tram lines and islands of fishing villages a ferry ride away. The contrast, of course, is the point. It is dense, vertical and electric, and at night from the Peak it is simply unreal.

Do not miss: the Peak at dusk, the Star Ferry across the harbour, dim sum in Central and the Dragon’s Back hike. Our 20 things you must do in Hong Kong has the rest.

Stay: The Peninsula, on the Kowloon waterfront since 1928, has the harbour view. The Landmark Mandarin Oriental, which we reviewed in our Landmark Mandarin Oriental review, is the Central choice.

Getting there: Hong Kong airport is 24 minutes from Central by Airport Express. Once there, Octopus cards work on everything.

15. Jaipur, India

Jaipur is the Pink City, a planned 18th-century capital of rose-coloured facades, palaces and bazaars, with a hilltop fort above and a palace floating in a lake. It is loud, dusty and chaotic, and yet it is the most beautiful city in India. Most importantly, it has the best palace hotels in the world.

Do not miss: Amber Fort at opening time, the Hawa Mahal from the café opposite, the Jantar Mantar observatory and the Johari Bazaar for block-printed textiles.

Stay: Rambagh Palace, a Taj hotel in the former Maharaja’s residence, is the one to book. Alternatively, Samode Haveli, a 175-year-old mansion in the old city, is the smaller, charming choice.

Getting there: Jaipur airport is 30 minutes from the old city. From Delhi, the train takes 4 hours 30 and the drive about 5.

16. Singapore

Singapore is the only city on this list that is beautiful because it was designed to be. Colonial shophouses sit under glass towers, 50-metre Supertrees light up at night in a garden by the bay and the airport has a waterfall. It is clean, green and safe, and as a result it is the best stopover on earth.

Do not miss: Gardens by the Bay at dusk, the infinity pool at Marina Bay Sands, hawker food in Maxwell Centre and a Singapore Sling in the Long Bar at Raffles.

Stay: Raffles Singapore, restored and still the grande dame of Asia, is the classic. Otherwise, Capella Singapore, on Sentosa, is the resort alternative.

Getting there: Changi airport is 20 minutes from the centre by taxi. Almost every long-haul route between Europe and Australia stops here.

17. Hoi An, Vietnam

Hoi An is a 16th-century trading port on a river in central Vietnam, with yellow-walled merchants’ houses, a Japanese covered bridge and thousands of silk lanterns lit at dusk. It is small, so it is here as the one small city on this list. It is also the best place in Asia to eat on a budget and have a suit made in 24 hours.

Do not miss: the old town at dawn before the tour groups, the lantern festival on the full moon, a cooking class and the beach at An Bang, 10 minutes away.

Stay: Four Seasons Resort The Nam Hai, on the beach between Hoi An and Da Nang, is the one to book. Alternatively, Anantara Hoi An, on the river, is the in-town choice.

Getting there: 45 minutes by car from Da Nang airport. Then, combine it with Hue and the Hai Van Pass.

The most beautiful cities in the world: Africa and Australia

Three cities, three harbours, and arguably the three most beautiful settings on the planet. Each one is a long flight, and each one is worth it.

18. Cape Town, South Africa

Cape Town has Table Mountain in the middle of it, two oceans either side, vineyards 20 minutes from downtown and beaches where penguins live. Simply put, no other city on earth has a setting like it. Add the Cape Dutch architecture, the food and the price, and it may be the most beautiful city in the world full stop.

Do not miss: the cable car up Table Mountain, sunset at Camps Bay, the penguins at Boulders Beach, Chapman’s Peak Drive and a day in the Constantia or Franschhoek vineyards. Our 20 best things to do in Cape Town has the rest.

Stay: The Silo, in a converted grain elevator on the waterfront, and Ellerman House, in Bantry Bay, are the two best hotels in Africa. We compared them in Ellerman House vs The Silo.

Getting there: Cape Town airport is 25 minutes from the city. Direct flights run from London, Amsterdam, Dubai, Doha and Singapore.

19. Sydney, Australia

Sydney is a harbour city first and everything else second. The Opera House and the Harbour Bridge are the icons, but equally the beauty is in the detail: a hundred coves and beaches, ferries criss-crossing all day, sandstone headlands and the light on the water. It is also where the best coastal walk in the world starts.

Do not miss: the Bondi to Coogee walk, the Manly ferry at sunset, a swim at Bronte baths and the Opera House from Mrs Macquarie’s Chair at dawn.

Stay: Capella Sydney, in the former Department of Education building near Circular Quay, is the newest grand hotel. Otherwise, Park Hyatt Sydney, under the bridge facing the Opera House, has the view.

Getting there: Sydney airport is 20 minutes from the centre by train. Direct flights run from Europe via Perth, and from most of Asia and North America.

20. Marrakech, Morocco

Marrakech is a walled red city at the foot of the snow-capped Atlas Mountains, with a medina of 9,000 alleyways, a square that turns into a street-food carnival every night and gardens hidden behind every door. It is overwhelming and intoxicating in equal measure. Then, inside a riad, it is utterly calm.

Do not miss: Jemaa el-Fnaa at dusk, the Jardin Majorelle at opening time, the Bahia Palace and the Ben Youssef Madrasa. Our cool places in Marrakech has the rest.

Stay: La Mamounia, with its 12-hectare gardens and Churchill’s old suite, is the classic. Royal Mansour, built by the King, is the most lavish hotel in Africa. See also our Oberoi Marrakech review.

Getting there: Menara airport is 20 minutes from the medina. In addition, direct flights run from across Europe in under 4 hours.

How to plan a trip around the most beautiful cities in the world

You cannot see all 20 in one trip, and you should not try. Instead, pick one continent and give each city at least three nights, because the best of every one of them is found on the third day. These four routes cover the best of the list.

When to go

There is no single answer, because the list spans both hemispheres. In general, April to June and September to October are best for Europe, the Americas and Asia. Cape Town and Sydney, on the other hand, are at their best from November to March. Kyoto is the exception, since April and November are its two peak weeks and need booking a year ahead.

Getting around

Every city on this list is walkable at its core, and most have excellent public transport. However, the distances between them are the real cost. Fly into one city and out of another, because backtracking wastes days. Our guide to Europe’s most walkable cities is a good companion for the European leg.

Budget

City hotels are the biggest cost, and they vary wildly. For example, expect €300 and up for a good hotel in Paris, Venice, New York, Hong Kong or Sydney, and half that in Lisbon, Prague, Istanbul, Jaipur, Hoi An and Marrakech. Our guide to travel costs in Europe has the European breakdown.

Frequently asked questions about the most beautiful cities in the world

Which are the most beautiful cities in the world overall?

Cape Town, Paris and Venice, if we have to pick three of the most beautiful cities in the world. Cape Town has the setting, Paris has the architecture and Venice has the improbability. Rio de Janeiro and Sydney, meanwhile, are close behind for their harbours.

What is the most beautiful city in Europe?

Paris for architecture and Venice for setting. Beyond those two, Prague and Lisbon are the ones most people underestimate. Our 30 most beautiful cities in Europe ranks them all.

Which of the most beautiful cities in the world are the cheapest to visit?

Hoi An, Jaipur, Marrakech, Istanbul, Prague and Lisbon. All six offer good hotels under €150 a night and excellent food for a few euros. Cape Town is also exceptional value for what you get.

When is the best time to visit the most beautiful cities in the world?

In short, spring and autumn for the northern hemisphere, and November to March for Cape Town, Sydney and Rio. Avoid July and August in Europe, and June to September in India and Southeast Asia, when the monsoon arrives.

Which of the most beautiful cities in the world are safest for solo travellers?

Singapore, Kyoto, Hong Kong, Vancouver, Sydney, Amsterdam and Edinburgh are the easiest. Our 26 safest cities for female solo travellers goes into detail.

How many days do you need in each of the most beautiful cities in the world?

Three nights is the minimum, and four is better. Paris, New York, Istanbul and Kyoto each deserve five, because they are big and layered. The smaller ones, such as Hoi An and Cartagena, work in two or three.
